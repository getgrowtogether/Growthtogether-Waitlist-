Submit well, not everywhere

How to work this directory without wasting a month

The list is the easy part. What separates the founders who get users from the ones who get silence is how each submission is made.

1

Build the submission kit once

A one-line description, a two-sentence version, a clean logo, real screenshots and a demo link that works without a login. Every site asks for the same six things — having them ready turns a week of submissions into two days.

2

Stagger — do not spray

Two or three careful submissions a week beat thirty in a day. Write each one for that site’s audience: a Product Hunt voter, a directory browser and a subreddit member are three different people, and copy-paste text reads as spam to all of them.

3

Show up on the day

On launch days and in communities, the submission is only the door opening. The users come from the comments — answer every question and criticism fast and in person. A founder who disappears after posting gets the traffic they deserve: almost none.

4

Track, then double down

Use a different tagged link per site so you can see which places sent people who stayed — not just people who clicked. After a month, drop the silent ones without sentiment and put your next effort into the two that worked. Questions people search for

Startup promotion, answered honestly

Where can I submit my startup for free?

Launch platforms like Product Hunt, Uneed, Microlaunch and TinyStartups take free submissions; directories like Startup Buffer, Startup Ranking and Launching Next list startups free after review; SaaSHub and AlternativeTo list software free; Indie Hackers and Show HN are free to post in. The directory above lists 26 of them with each one’s free route and limit.

What is the best place to promote a startup?

It depends on the stage. For one launch day, a launch platform. For discovery that keeps dripping in, directories and software listings. For users who actually return, communities and cross-promotion. Most startups need all three, in that order — and none of them replaces having something people want.

How many directories should I submit my startup to?

Ten to fifteen, done properly, beat a hundred copy-paste submissions. Bulk-submission services sell volume; what they cannot sell is the hour you spend answering comments on launch day, which is where the users actually come from.

Do startup directories actually bring users?

A drip, not a flood — a few visitors a day from the good ones, building over months as the listing itself starts appearing in search results. Their honest value is steady discovery plus a public record that your startup exists.
