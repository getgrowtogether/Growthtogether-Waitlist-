The loop

Give discovery. Get discovery.

Ranking on GrowTogether is not a mystery score and not an auction. It is one fair loop, the same for a worldwide brand and a day-one project.

1

Submit your project — and it is reviewed first

Every listing passes a safety and quality review before it appears: is it real, does it work, is it what it claims to be? Listings that are deceptive, copied, illegal or harmful are refused. A project that passes enters the rotation; a project that fails is told why.

2

Discover five projects from other members

You are shown five projects, matched to what you care about. Open them, try them, and leave honest feedback when you have something real to say. Genuine discovery is what earns credits — opening a page and closing it in a second is not discovery.

3

Your credits put your project in the rotation

Credits earned by discovering others move your project into the fair rotation, where it is shown to members around the world. Every approved project gets its turn — the rotation is the ranking.

The rules of the rotation

Fair means the same rules for everyone

1

New projects get a turn

A project does not need an audience, reviews or history to be shown. Every approved listing enters the rotation — being new is not a penalty.

2

Matching is by fit, not by size

Projects are shown to members whose interests fit them — a project for creators is shown to creators, not to everyone at random. A relevant audience that stays is worth more than a large audience that bounces.

3

Nobody can buy a place in the rotation

There is no paid priority, no boosted listing and no way to pay for a better position — the whole platform is free, and paid priority inside the free rotation will never be sold.

4

Invite links earn earlier beta access — never credits

Bringing genuine builders through your invite link moves you earlier into the beta. It never earns discovery credits and never moves your project in the rotation. Invites are for people, not for position.

5

Farming is removed, not rewarded

Bots, auto-surfing, fake accounts, tabs left open and click rings are filtered out by integrity checks. Sessions that fail the checks are not counted, and accounts that farm are removed from the circle.

Honest numbers

Every number says exactly what it is

Old traffic exchanges died because their counters lied. On GrowTogether, three numbers are counted separately and labelled as what they are — so a number on your dashboard means exactly what it says, and nothing more.

1

Card views

How many times your project card was shown to a member. A view is not a visit — it only means someone saw it.

2

Clicks

How many members opened your project. A click is not a customer — it only means someone chose to look.

3

Genuine sessions

Visits that passed the integrity checks — a real person who stayed and looked. Only these count towards discovery credit.

What ranking never promises

No honest platform can promise customers

Ranking in the circle guarantees one thing: real people discovering your project, and a fair chance to be found. It does not promise customers, sales, signups, a Google ranking or viral reach — and any platform that promises those things for free is not telling you the truth. What your project does with the chance is yours.

Questions

Ranking, answered plainly

Does a bigger project rank higher than mine?

No. The rotation gives every approved project its turn, and matching is by fit, not by size. A day-one project and a worldwide brand follow exactly the same rule: give discovery, get discovery.

Can I pay to be shown first?

No — there is nothing to pay for. GrowTogether has no plans and no pricing, and paid priority inside the free rotation will never be sold.

What happens if someone farms credits?

Their sessions fail the integrity checks and are not counted. Accounts that farm — bots, fake accounts, auto-surfing — are removed from the circle.

Do invite links help my ranking?

No. Invite links only move you earlier into the beta. They never earn discovery credits and never change your place in the rotation.

Why was my project refused in review?

Because it failed the safety or quality review — for example it did not work, was not what it claimed to be, was copied from someone else, or broke the rules in the Terms. Refused listings are told the reason, and can be fixed and submitted again.
