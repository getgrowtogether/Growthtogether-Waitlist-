The classic list — with its limits

Free ways to promote a website (and what each one really gives you)

These methods are real, and most builders should use several of them. But read the honest line under each — this is the part the listicles leave out.

1

Search engines & listings

Set up Search Console, submit your sitemap, claim your business and directory listings — Google Business Profile and Bing Places matter most, and our free advertising sites directory checks 37 more by hand. The honest limit: a slow base that takes months to matter — necessary, never fast.

2

Launch platforms & directories

Launch days and directory listings put you in front of early adopters — Product Hunt is the famous one, and our startup directory checks 25 places with each one’s free route and limit. The honest limit: one burst, then silence — a launch is a day, not a system.

3

Communities & forums

Reddit, niche forums and maker communities reward genuine participation. The honest limit: weeks of contributing before anyone clicks. Read each community’s own rules first — many ban self-promotion outright; expect karma and account-age gates, contribute before you promote, and say plainly that the project is yours.

4

Content & SEO

Articles that answer real questions compound for years — the guides at the bottom of this page are this route, worked in public. The honest limit: you are writing for an audience you do not have yet, and Google decides when it starts working.

5

Social profiles

Posting where your audience already scrolls costs nothing — a YouTube channel, an Instagram page, a TikTok account. The honest limit: a new profile speaks to an empty room — reach belongs to people who already have reach.

6

Cross-promotion

Partner with someone at your level and recommend each other — two newsletters swapping a recommendation is the classic shape, and our cross-promotion guide shows how to run one fairly. Guides have recommended this for years because it works. The honest limit: finding partners, negotiating and tracking fairness by hand does not scale — until someone builds the circle that runs it for everyone.
