Free advertising sites, by what they are for

A free advertising site is only worth your time if real people still browse it. Every entry names what it is best for, what the free tier actually gives you, and where the free part ends. Advertising sites for free are not all the same — a local listing, a classified ad and a product launch are three different jobs. Work through one group at a time, in the order your project needs it, and use the “How to use this directory” section below the listings if you are not sure where your project belongs.

Local listings — be found where people search nearby

A free business listing is not an ad in the classic sense, but it is where most local customers find a business — and a complete profile costs nothing. These eight are the ones worth your details: the three map-and-search profiles first, then the review and neighbourhood sites your customers actually open.

  • Local listing

    Google Business Profile

    Best for: every local business. Free tier: full listing on Google Search and Maps, reviews, posts. Honest limit: verification takes days; the ads above your listing are paid. Fill in every field — categories, hours, services, photos — because the completeness is the work: a half-empty profile is the version of free that does not work.

    Visit site
  • Local listing

    Bing Places for Business

    Best for: extra search coverage with less competition. Free tier: full listing on Bing Search and Maps. Honest limit: less traffic than Google, slower verification. If you already keep a Google profile, the same details carry over — but the name, address and hours must match it exactly, or both listings suffer.

    Visit site
  • Local listing

    Apple Business Connect

    Best for: reaching iPhone, Siri and Apple Maps users. Free tier: full place card with photos, hours and actions. Honest limit: verification wait; Apple users only. Worth doing even if your customers feel Android-heavy: Apple Maps is the default on every iPhone, and its place cards are what Siri reads out.

    Visit site
  • Local listing

    Yelp

    Best for: restaurants, salons, spas, local services. Free tier: business listing with reviews and photos. Honest limit: paid placements are pushed hard above the free listing. Answer reviews, briefly and politely — on Yelp your response sits directly under the review, and it is part of what the next customer reads.

  • Local listing

    Nextdoor

    Best for: neighbourhood word of mouth. Free tier: free business page and local posts. Honest limit: reach is local by design; promoted posts are paid. Neighbours recommend people they feel they know: a business that answers local questions and shows up in the feed like a neighbour, not a billboard, is the one that gets named when someone asks.

Treat it as plumbing, not promotion: its value is one more consistent copy of your name, address and phone number on a site search engines have trusted for decades.

Classifieds — free ads posting sites for goods, jobs and services

The classic free advertising sites. Post a listing, local buyers browse it daily. The cost is your time — and on some of them, a fee in a few categories. Pick the one your country actually uses; classifieds are stubbornly local, and the leader in one country is a ghost town in another.

  • Classifieds

    Craigslist

    Best for: local goods, jobs, housing and services. Free tier: free in most categories, across hundreds of city sites. Honest limit: in the US, service ads, dealer ads and cars by owner cost $5, jobs $10–$75; one post per category every 48 hours. Write the post like a person, not a catalogue: one clear title, real photos, a specific price. Vague posts are the ones that get flagged or ignored.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    Facebook Marketplace

    Best for: household goods and rentals, local pickup. Free tier: free to list for local pickup. Honest limit: a 10% fee (minimum $0.80) applies to shipped orders. Buyers can see your profile’s history, which cuts both ways — a real profile with a face sells; a fresh, empty one gets scrolled past.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    ClassifiedAds.com

    Best for: quick classified posts. Free tier: free, and no registration is needed to post. Honest limit: plain listings — no built-in audience beyond search. With no account wall, posts go live fast — and age fast. Renew on the rhythm the site allows instead of posting duplicates.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    Locanto

    Best for: services, events and local announcements in 60+ countries. Free tier: free ads; paid boosts are optional. Honest limit: listing quality varies a lot by city. Strong in parts of Europe, Latin America and Asia where Craigslist never landed; check your own city’s activity before investing an hour — an empty local section helps nobody.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    Gumtree

    Best for: UK goods, jobs, property and services. Free tier: free in most categories. Honest limit: UK-focused; thin elsewhere. In the UK it plays the role Craigslist plays in the US — the default place locals look first — so a clear title and honest photos do the whole job.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    OLX

    Best for: used goods, cars and property across 30+ countries. Free tier: free in most categories. Honest limit: strong in some countries, thin in others. In the countries where it is strong, it is often the first marketplace people open; where it is thin, the same hour is better spent on the local leader instead.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    Oodle

    Best for: aggregated classified reach, including pets. Free tier: free to post. Honest limit: it aggregates, so your ad competes with copies of itself. Because it pulls listings from elsewhere, check how yours appears here even if you never post — an aggregated copy with a wrong price or a dead link is still your listing in a stranger’s eyes.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    Geebo

    Best for: general moderated listings in the US. Free tier: free to post. Honest limit: modest traffic compared with the big classifieds. The moderation is the feature: listings are screened, which makes it a calmer shelf than the open classifieds — worth an hour if your category attracts spam everywhere else.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    Hoobly

    Best for: pets, events and local services. Free tier: free to post. Honest limit: a niche audience, not a general one. For pet breeders and event organisers it is a genuine community rather than a billboard; the same focus that limits its audience is what makes that audience real.

    Visit site
  • Classifieds

    Best for: India — goods, jobs, homes and services. Free tier: free in most categories. Honest limit: India-focused; thin elsewhere. For Indian cities it covers the everyday categories — rentals, used goods, local services, jobs — in one account, which makes it the efficient first listing for an Indian project.

Marketplaces — free to list, a cut only when you sell

Listing costs nothing; the platform earns when the sale happens. That is the honest trade — read the selling fee before you price anything, and price with the fee and the shipping already counted. Marketplaces reward complete listings with real photos and fast answers, and quietly bury the rest.

  • Marketplace

    eBay

    Best for: products with a worldwide buyer base. Free tier: 250 free listings a month. Honest limit: after that, about 13.6% plus $0.30–$0.40 per sale. Price against completed listings, not active ones: what things actually sold for is public, and it is the only price research that matters here.

    Visit site
  • Marketplace

    OfferUp

    Best for: electronics, collectibles and fashion in the US. Free tier: free to list locally. Honest limit: fees apply to shipped sales. Buyer and seller ratings sit on every profile, so your first few careful, fast-answered sales matter more than your tenth listing — they set the score strangers judge you by.

    Visit site
  • Marketplace

    Mercari

    Best for: shippable items, US. Free tier: free to list. Honest limit: a selling fee is charged on each sale. Listing from a phone takes minutes, which is its real appeal; the discipline is pricing with the selling fee and shipping already counted, not discovered afterwards.

    Visit site
  • Marketplace

    Kijiji

    Best for: Canada — goods, jobs, property, services. Free tier: free in most categories. Honest limit: Canada-focused; thin elsewhere. Canada’s default classifieds habit: for anything local in Canada — a room, a car, a service — this is the listing that cannot be skipped.

    Visit site

    Product Hunt

    Best for: launching a product to early adopters. Free tier: free launch page. Honest limit: launch-day competition is fierce; you get one big shot per product. The day is won in the weeks before it: a coming-soon page collecting followers, a first comment that tells the real story, and your existing audience told plainly where to be.

Social & community — free reach, earned one post at a time

Posting is free everywhere here. The price is consistency — and following each community's rules, or your posts get removed. None of these pays off in a weekend; all of them pay off in a season, which is exactly why most projects abandon them too early.

  • Social

    LinkedIn Page

    Best for: reaching a professional audience. Free tier: free company page and posts. Honest limit: organic reach is thin without real engagement. A page with no posts is a dead end. The free reach on LinkedIn lives in people — founders and staff posting from their own profiles and pointing at the page.

    Visit site
  • Social

    Pinterest

    Best for: visual products and ideas that keep bringing traffic for months. Free tier: free business account and pins. Honest limit: needs visual content, posted consistently. Treat pins as search results, not posts: name each image with the words a buyer would type, and link every pin to the exact page it shows.

    Visit site
  • Social

    Reddit communities

    Best for: honest feedback and early users who care about the topic. Free tier: free to post where the rules allow it. Honest limit: each community has its own rules; self-promotion without contribution gets removed. Lurk first, answer second, link last — in that order, over weeks. The accounts that get value from Reddit are the ones that would still be there with nothing to sell.

    Visit site
  • Social

    Quora

    Best for: answers that rank on Google for years. Free tier: free to answer. Honest limit: answers must genuinely help; links alone get collapsed. One thorough answer to a question your buyers actually ask can outrank blog posts for years; write the answer you would want to find, and let the profile carry the link.

How to use this directory

Thirty-seven sites is a toolbox, not a to-do list. The right three depend on what you are promoting — find your row below, and do those first.

A

A place people visit — shop, café, clinic, local service

Start with local listings, in this order: Google Business Profile, then Bing Places and Apple Business Connect in the same afternoon. Those three are most of the local game. Add Yelp if your customers read reviews before they visit, and Nextdoor if your work travels by neighbourhood word of mouth.

B

Something to sell — goods, a car, a room, a service

Start with classifieds for local pickup — Craigslist or your country’s equivalent — and marketplaces for anything shippable. Read the selling fee before you set a price, not after the first sale. One honest, well-photographed listing per site beats five rushed ones.

C

Something new — an app, a website, a SaaS, a channel

Start with launch & discovery, but split it in two: listings you can plant any day (AlternativeTo, SaaSHub, the review profiles) and launch days you get once (Product Hunt, Show HN). Plant the listings this week; save the launch days until the project can survive the attention — and if you are still near zero users, the first 100 users guide is where to build that first audience.

D

An audience — followers, members, subscribers

Start with social & community: the platforms where your people already spend time, posted to consistently, plus a review profile so strangers who check you find proof. Communities pay slowly and honestly — they reward the accounts that were useful before they had anything to promote.

F

A first afternoon, worked through

Say you run a neighbourhood bakery. Hour one: Google Business Profile, completed fully — hours, photos of the real counter, the menu link, the category set to bakery and not just restaurant. Hour two: Bing Places and Apple Business Connect with the identical details, copied carefully, not retyped from memory. Hour three: claim the Yelp profile, answer the two reviews already sitting there, and post one classified in the local section for the weekend’s special. That is the whole local game started in an afternoon — three finished profiles and one live ad beat a month of intending to list on thirty sites.

E

What to do first, whichever row is yours

Use the search box and the category chips above to shortlist three to five sites. Then complete the first profile fully — every field, real photos, the same name, address and words you use everywhere else — before you open the second. A finished profile on one site outperforms five skeletons. Write each listing fresh for that site’s reader; the copy-paste version reads like spam because it is.

Use the directory well

How to use free advertising sites without wasting weeks

Free means you pay with time. Spend it where it returns something.

1

Pick by goal, not by list size

Local customers: start with the local listings. Selling things: start with the classifieds and marketplaces. Launching something new: start with the launch directories. Three to five sites, done properly, beat forty thin copy-paste listings.

2

Write each listing for that site

A Craigslist buyer, a Product Hunt voter and a Google searcher are three different people. Same project, different words, real photos, a clear next step. A listing copied word-for-word everywhere reads like spam — and gets treated like it.

3

Keep it fresh, then measure

Renew or repost on the schedule each site allows, answer every reply fast, and track which site actually sent people — not which one felt busy. Drop the sites that send nothing after a month, and double down on the one or two that do.

4

Know what free cannot do

Free listings bring a slow drip, not a flood — honest guides put it at tens to a few hundred visits, built over months. The missing piece is people who promote you because you promote them. That is what the circle below is for.

Honest expectations

What free advertising can — and cannot — do

Free advertising is real, and it is also routinely oversold. Here is the honest shape of it, so you can plan around the truth instead of the pitch.

✓

What it can do

It puts you in the places people already look: the map, the classifieds page, the launch list, the review profile a stranger checks before trusting you. It compounds quietly — a complete local listing keeps answering searches for years, and every honest review you collect stays collected. And it costs no money, which for a new project is sometimes the whole point. Directory and review pages are also indexed by search engines themselves, so a good listing can be found twice: once by the site’s own browsers, and again by everyone who searches the site’s name alongside your category.

✕

What it cannot do

It will not flood you: expect a drip — tens to a few hundred visits, built over months — not a launch-day wave. It will not fix a weak offer: free visitors arriving at a page that does not convince them are just free disappointment. And it will not stay done: profiles decay, fees change, posts age out, and a listing you never revisit slowly stops being true.

✓

What it cannot do for your Google ranking

A listing is not a ranking lever. No directory above will lift your website up Google’s results by itself, whatever the listing was sold as. What listings genuinely do is put you in front of that site’s own audience — and, for local businesses, consistent name, address and phone details across listings support the local presence that shows in Maps. Buy listings for audiences, never for rankings.

✕

The mistakes that make free worthless

Copy-pasting one text into thirty sites, so every listing reads like it was written for nobody. Listing and never returning — no review answered, no photo updated, no post renewed — until the profile is a museum of who you were. And measuring nothing: free sites feel busy at very different rates from how they actually send people, and the only cure is one trackable link or one honest question — “how did you find us?” — asked of everyone who arrives.

✕

The real price: your hours

Every profile wants completing, checking and renewing, and the hours are the price list nobody prints. Budget for them the way you would budget money: a few focused hours to set up your three to five sites properly, then a short monthly pass to answer reviews, refresh photos and check that every fee and fact is still what the listing claims. That rhythm — not posting everywhere at once — is what makes free advertising pay.

Free advertising sites, answered honestly

What are the best free advertising sites?

The ones with a real audience behind the free tier: Google Business Profile, Bing Places and Apple Business Connect for local search; Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace and ClassifiedAds.com for classifieds; eBay for products; Product Hunt and Show HN for launching something new — BetaList, the third famous launch name, went paid in late 2026 and is marked as such below. The directory above lists 37 of them, each with what is free, what is not, and its honest limit.

Is Craigslist still free to post an ad?

Mostly. Posting is free in most categories, but in the US some posts are charged: service ads, dealer ads and cars by owner cost $5, job ads $10 to $75, and gigs $3 to $10 in some areas. Check the fee shown on the posting page for your city and category before you publish.

Are free advertising sites actually free?

The listing is free on all but one site in this directory — that part is real, and the one exception is marked in its entry. What is not free is your time, and on some of them the leads: marketplaces take a cut when something sells, and directories sell promoted placements above the free listing. A free site is worth it only if real people still browse it, which is why every site in this directory is one real people still browse.

How many free advertising sites should I post on?

Three to five, done properly, beat forty thin copy-paste listings. Pick the ones that match your goal — local listings for local customers, classifieds for selling things, launch directories for a new product — write each listing for that site's audience, and keep it fresh.

Does GrowTogether charge for promotion?

No. There are no plans and no pricing — the whole platform is free. Members contribute honest attention instead of money.

How long until free listings start bringing visitors?

It depends on the kind of site. A classified ad in an active city can bring replies the same day. A completed local listing can appear in search and maps within days of verification, then keep working for years. Directory and launch listings are slower — judge them after a month, not a morning. The pattern to expect is a drip that grows as your profiles complete and your reviews collect, not a spike.

Do directory listings help my Google ranking?

Not in the way they are often sold. A listing is not a ranking lever: no directory on this page will lift a website up Google’s results by itself. What listings do is put you in front of that site’s own audience — and, for a local business, consistent name, address and phone details across listings support the local presence Google shows in Maps. Anyone who sells a listing as a ranking shortcut is selling the listing, not the ranking.

What is the catch with free advertising sites?

Your time, mostly: every profile wants completing, checking and renewing, and the hours are the real price. After that, the free listing usually sits below paid placements on the same page, marketplaces take a fee when something sells, and a few categories that look free are charged — Craigslist job ads are the famous example. None of that makes the free tier fake; it means free is a starting position, not the whole game.
