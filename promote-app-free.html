The short answer

Fix the store listing first — ASO decides whether anyone who finds your app understands it. Then launch once, properly, on Product Hunt, post the app in the free places where new apps are hunted, and show up in the communities and feeds where your users already are.

The free routes — with their limits

Free app promotion, route by route

The routes below cost time, not money — with one named exception: a famous listing place in the list below made submissions a paid service in late 2026, and its entry says so plainly. Each route also has a ceiling — read it before you spend weeks on the wrong one. One honest note first: no public source publishes a search volume for the exact phrase "how to promote your app for free", so this page makes no volume claim — the demand is real, shown by fresh ranking pages from independent sites, and the routes below are the ones those pages actually recommend.

1

ASO: make the store listing findable before anything else

App Store Optimisation is search for apps: a name people can remember, a subtitle that carries the words people type, a description that says what the app does in the first two lines, and screenshots that show the result — not the settings screen — in the first three seconds. The honest limit: ASO only helps people who already search the store for your kind of app; it brings no one who has never heard of the category.

2

Launch day: Product Hunt, done properly, once

Product Hunt is where new products are hunted daily: a clear tagline, real screenshots, a short video of the app working, and the maker answering every comment on launch day. The honest limit: you get one real launch day — a weak listing burns it, and a good launch brings a spike of curious visitors, not a permanent stream of users.

3

The 9 places to post your app

Directories and launch sites list new apps by category and keep sending visitors long after you submit — the full list with each place's limit is below. The honest limit: a listing is a signpost, not a crowd; most of these places send a trickle, and the free queue on the popular ones is slow.

4

Communities: Reddit and the places your users already gather

Post where your users already are — subreddits for your problem, not your app — and lead with the problem you solved, not a download link. A post that helps first and mentions the app second gets kept; a bare link gets removed. The honest limit: every community has rules against self-promotion, and breaking them once can get you muted there for good.

5

Build in public: X, LinkedIn and Indie Hackers

Show the app being built — the bug, the fix, the first user, the number that moved — a little every day, where your users already scroll. People install apps from makers they have watched. The honest limit: this is a habit for months, not a launch trick; a week of posts buys almost nothing.

6

Cross-promotion: other developers are your best channel

Two apps with neighbouring audiences recommend each other — inside the app, in a newsletter, in a launch post. The users you gain already install this kind of app. The honest limit: finding partners and keeping it fair by hand does not scale — until a circle runs it for everyone.

7

What never works: bought installs, fake reviews, forced installs

Bought installs are fake accounts — the stores detect them and can remove the app. Fake reviews poison trust with the real users you do reach, and forced installs bring people who uninstall in a day, which tells the store your app is not worth showing. Promotion that lasts is pull: real people who chose to install.

9 places to post your app

Free app promotion sites, with their limits

Submit to all of them — almost every one is free to list on, and the one that went paid says so in its entry — but read the limit first, so you know what each listing can and cannot do for you.

1

Product Hunt

The biggest launch day for new products. Free to launch; the maker answers comments all day. Limit: one real launch day, and a spike, not a stream.

2

BetaList

Lists pre-launch and newly launched startups and apps. Limit: submissions became a paid service in late 2026 — it stays on this list because founders still search for it, but check the current terms before counting on a free route; the audience is founders, not end users.

3

AlternativeTo

People searching for an alternative to a big app find yours here. Free to list. Limit: only helps if your app is a real alternative to something people already search for.

4

Indie Hackers

Founders share products and numbers in public. Free to post. Limit: the audience is builders, so it brings feedback and partners more than end users.

5

Hacker News — Show HN

Show your work to a technical audience that loves new tools. Free to post. Limit: a harsh audience; a post that reads like an ad sinks fast.

6

Reddit communities

Subreddits for your problem — r/SideProject, r/alphaandbetausers and the community for your niche. Free to post. Limit: strict self-promotion rules; help first, link second.

7

SaaSHub

A directory where people compare software and apps by category. Free to list. Limit: a trickle of visitors, not a flood — it compounds over months.

8

X — build in public

Daily posts about building the app, where your users already scroll. Free to post. Limit: needs months of consistency; one thread changes nothing.

9

LinkedIn

Short posts about the problem and the fix, aimed at the people who feel it at work. Free to post. Limit: works for work tools; weak for consumer apps.

Not counted above: Capterra and G2. Both are business-software review sites. Buyers read them before choosing a tool, but neither links out to your store page and neither sends consumer downloads. If your app is a business tool, a profile there is long-term proof for buyers who research you — build it, but do not count it as promotion to users.

A plan you can actually follow

Your first 100 downloads in 14 days

This is how to promote my app for free, step by step — and how to get your first 100 downloads with no budget and no viral spike: ten real installs a day, from places where people are already looking.

1–2

Days 1–2: fix the store listing

Rewrite the subtitle with the words people type, redo the first three screenshots to show the result, and ask three real users what the app does in one sentence — use their words, not yours.

3–5

Days 3–5: prepare one launch

Prepare the Product Hunt listing — tagline, screenshots, a 30-second video of the app working — and submit to AlternativeTo and SaaSHub the same week, so the listings are live when launch day comes.

6–7

Days 6–7: launch day

Launch, answer every comment yourself, and post the launch in two communities where you have already helped people — not ten places where nobody knows you.

8–14

Days 8–14: build in public, every day

One short post a day about what changed — the fix, the first review, the number that moved — and one honest conversation a day with a user. At ten installs a day, the first 100 arrive before the two weeks are over, and they are users who chose the app.

How can I promote my app for free?

Use the routes that cost time, not money: make the store listing findable (ASO), launch on Product Hunt, post it in the 9 places that list new apps, share it in communities that allow it, build in public where your users already are, and swap promotion with other developers. How do I promote an app with no budget?

Start with the store listing itself — a clear name, a keyword-rich subtitle, screenshots that show the result in the first three seconds — then launch it in one place where new apps are hunted daily, and tell the story in public for two weeks. Paid ads only speed up a message that already works; with no budget, the message has to work first.

How do I get my first 100 downloads?

The first 100 downloads almost never come from the store alone. They come from places where people are already looking for new apps: a launch day, a directory listing, a community post that solves a real problem, and other developers recommending your app to their users. Aim for ten installs a day from real conversations, not one viral spike.

Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, Indie Hackers, Hacker News Show HN, SaaSHub, Capterra and G2 all let you list or launch an app without paying, and Reddit communities and build-in-public posts cost nothing but time.
