The short answer

There is no switch that sends visitors to a new website. The honest answer to how to get traffic to your website free is this: go where people already are. Answer real questions in one community your readers use, list your site in directories people actually browse, publish pages that answer one specific search question at a time, and swap discovery with other site owners. Communities and directories can act within days; search takes weeks to months. Free means you pay with time instead of money — and no method guarantees traffic.

The empty dashboard

“I built a website. Nobody visits it. What did I do wrong?”

That sentence, in one form or another, fills the forums where site owners ask for help. The wording changes; the feeling does not. If any of these sound familiar, this page was written for you.

1

“Why is my website not getting any traffic after a month?”

You did what everyone said: you built it properly, you launched, you waited. A month later the visitor counter has barely moved. The honest answer: launching is not distribution. A website is not a billboard on a busy road — it is a shop on a street nobody knows exists yet, and nobody hands you traffic just for existing.

2

“It has been more than six months and still not a single real visitor”

Six months of silence feels like proof the site is broken. Usually nothing is broken. The site was never connected to any road that leads to it: no page answers a question people actually type, nobody links to it, and it has never been shown to an audience that already exists. Indexed is not the same as found — a search engine can know your site exists and still never pick it.

3

“Most of the visits in my analytics are me”

One owner counted eleven visitors in just over three weeks — and most of them were their own checks of the analytics page. That sting is the real starting point for almost every site that later grew: the moment you stop redesigning the page nobody sees and start feeding one route that brings people to it. If your project is a website first, our guide to free website promotion covers the same ground from the website owner's side.

4

“I posted my link everywhere and got removed”

The other common story: a link dropped into a forum on day one, deleted by a moderator; the same link in a group, ignored. That is not a traffic method failing — it is the absence of one. Every visitor on every site arrived through a road somebody built: a search result, a link from another site, a community where the owner is known, or an audience that comes back on its own. The fix is to build one road properly, not to shout down all of them.

The honest truth about time

No method works overnight — here is what each one really takes

Most pages about free traffic skip the timelines, because the timelines are not exciting. They are also the only part that decides whether you keep going or quit in week two. Read these three before you choose a method.

1

Search is the slowest road — and the one that lasts

A new site is invisible to search by default. Pages need to be found, indexed and tested against everything already ranking, and that takes weeks before anything settles and months before search becomes a steady source. That is not a flaw in your site; it is how a new domain earns trust. Start search content early, judge it after months, and never build your whole plan on it for this month.

2

Communities and directories act in days

A helpful answer in a busy community can send visitors the same day, because you are borrowing an audience that already exists instead of waiting to build one. A directory listing works the same way, more quietly: it sits where people already browse and sends a trickle while you sleep. The trade: borrowed audiences have rules, ceilings and moods — they start fast and they do not compound the way search does.

3

What “free” really costs

Free traffic is not free. You do not pay with money; you pay with hours — hours to write the page, answer the question, keep the listing current and show up again next week. Anyone who prices those hours honestly sometimes finds paid ads are the rational choice, and that is fine. What is not fine is a page that calls a method free while hiding the tool subscription, the credit pack or the thirty hours behind it. On this page, every method states its effort in plain words.

The methods, ranked

How to get traffic to your website free: 5 methods, ranked by return on your time

Lists of thirty ways to get free traffic are easy to write and impossible to act on — nobody does thirty things. These five are ranked by what they return for the hours they cost, fastest honest return first. Do two or three properly rather than all five thinly.

1

Communities where your people already are

Find the one or two places your readers already gather — the subreddit for your topic, the forum for your trade, the question site where your problem gets asked — and become a regular there, not a poster of links. Answer questions completely, inside the platform, in full. Where a link genuinely helps, add it after the answer, as further reading. A bare link on day one gets removed; a complete answer from a known name gets read, saved and followed. Our guide to promoting on Reddit without getting banned shows the rules that keep this route open. Effort: about 20 minutes a day, most days. The honest limit: every community has self-promotion rules, and breaking them once can close the place to you for good.

2

Free directories and listings people actually browse

List your site in the directories for your kind of project — the category pages, launch boards and curated lists that a real person would browse when looking for something like yours. Write each listing in the reader's words, not yours: what it does, who it is for, what it costs them (nothing, if that is true). Then stop. The test for any directory is simple: would a human ever browse it? Our directory of free advertising sites works through the listing places worth your time, with the limit of each one stated. Effort: about 2 hours to set up the first batch, then 20 minutes when you add a new page. The honest limit: a listing is a signpost, not a crowd — expect a trickle that compounds, not a flood.

3

Long-tail search content: one page, one real question

This is the long game, and the highest ceiling on this page. Find the exact questions your readers type — long, specific phrases with clear intent, the kind a new site can realistically rank for — and write one page that answers one question completely. Put the answer in the first lines, use the words people type rather than the words you prefer, link your pages to each other where it helps the reader, and check in Search Console which queries already show your site, so you can strengthen the pages that nearly rank. Two to four honest pages a month beat a page a day of thin filler. Effort: about 3 hours for the first page, then one page a week. The honest limit: nothing for weeks, then a slow build over months — start it in week one precisely because it is slow.

4

Cross-promotion and the discovery circle

Other owners have the audience you need, and you have theirs. Two sites with neighbouring readers recommend each other — a link in a guide, a mention in a newsletter, a shared post — and both sides gain visitors who were already interested in this kind of thing. Done by hand, it works and does not scale: finding partners, agreeing terms and checking the swap stayed fair eats the hours it was meant to save. That is exactly the problem a discovery circle exists to solve, by turning the swap into one fair system for everyone in it — which is what GrowTogether is being built to do, and the pitch section below explains how. Effort: an afternoon to find and write to the first partners, then an hour a week to keep swaps honest. The honest limit: partners must be real and relevant — a swap with an unrelated site sends visitors who leave in seconds.

5

Social proof loops: milestones and public answers

People follow progress they can see. Share the milestone when it happens — the first reader who was not you, the fix a visitor asked for, the number that moved — in the places your readers already scroll, and answer questions publicly wherever they are asked, so each answer becomes a page that keeps working. One good piece of work becomes several honest posts: the finding, the method, the mistake and the question it answers. Effort: about 20 minutes to turn one piece of work into posts, a few times a week. The honest limit: this is a habit measured in months; a week of posts buys almost nothing, and broadcasting the same link everywhere with no presence buys less than nothing, because it spends the goodwill the other methods need.

What the ranking pages leave out

The “best free traffic sites” lists rarely warn you about these

Search for the best free traffic sites and the top results mix honest routes with offers that cost you money, trust or your ad account. These four are the ones to walk past — knowing them saves more time than any method above.

1

Traffic exchanges and surf-for-credits sites

You view other members' sites to earn credits, and your site is shown to people doing the same. The visits are incentivised, not interested: nobody arrives looking for what you made, so nobody stays, buys or returns. Worse, sending incentivised visits to pages carrying adverts can breach the ad network's rules and put the account at risk. A graph that moves is not the same as an audience.

2

“Free” tools that are paid tools in disguise

A common pattern on ranking pages: the method is free, the tool the method quietly requires is not — the free tier ends after the first report, the first list or the first week. Check what the full method costs before you build a routine around it. Every method on this page can be done with tools that cost nothing: a search console, a document and your own time.

3

Bought visitors, followers and clicks

Bought traffic does not convert, does not stay and does not fool the platforms for long — search engines and ad networks are better at spotting it than the sellers claim. It also poisons your own numbers: once fake visits are mixed into your analytics, you can no longer tell whether a real method is working.

4

Directory blasts and comment links

Submitting to hundreds of directories nobody reads produces hundreds of links nobody follows. Dropping links into blog comments has been worthless for years — the links are marked to be ignored and moderators remove them. Ten listings in places people browse beat a thousand submissions to places they do not.

A plan you can actually follow

Your first 30 days: a week-by-week plan to get free traffic

Thirty days will not make search love you, and this plan does not pretend otherwise. It will connect your site to every fast road, start the slow one, and leave you with a routine that fits inside a few hours a week — the same steady build that later carries a site towards its first 100 users.

W1

Week 1: be findable, then be listed

Set up Search Console, confirm your important pages are indexed, and search for your own site the way a stranger would. Fix anything that blocks a crawler or confuses a reader. Then submit your site to the first batch of directories people actually browse, using the listing wording you wrote once and reuse everywhere.

W2

Week 2: join one community properly

Pick the single community where your readers are most active. Read its rules, read its best posts, and answer questions in full for the whole week without posting a single link. By the end of the week your name is recognised, which is the asset every later post depends on.

W3

Week 3: publish the first search page

Write the page that answers the most specific question your readers type — the one you have now heard in the community with your own ears. Answer it completely, in their words, and share the answer in the community as a full answer with the page as further reading. Write to two owners of neighbouring sites about a cross-promotion swap the same week.

W4

Week 4: turn the month into a routine

Publish the second search page, share one honest milestone post, and look at the month in Search Console and your analytics: which road sent real people, and which sent none? Keep the roads that worked, drop the ones that did not, and repeat the routine. The plan from here is not a new plan — it is this month, done again, with the search pages compounding quietly underneath.

Questions people search for

Free traffic, answered honestly

How can I get free traffic to my website?

Start where people already are. Answer real questions in one community your readers use, list your site in directories people actually browse, and publish pages that answer one specific search question at a time, in the words people type. Then swap discovery with other site owners through cross-promotion. None of it costs money, but all of it costs time, and search is the slowest part, so start it early and judge it after months, not days.

What are the best free traffic sites?

The best free traffic sites are the places your readers already use: the community or subreddit for your topic, the question sites where your problem gets asked, and the directories for your kind of project that a person would actually browse. A short list you work properly beats a long list of exchanges and submission sites nobody reads. Incentivised traffic exchanges are not on that list, because the visits are not real interest and they can put an ad account at risk.

How long does it take to get free traffic?

Communities and directories can send the first visitors within days, because you are borrowing an audience that already exists. Search takes longer: a new site usually needs weeks before pages settle and months before search becomes a steady source. No method guarantees traffic, and any page that promises visitors overnight is selling something.

Is free traffic really free?

It is free in money and not free in time. You pay with hours: writing the page, answering the question, keeping the listing current and showing up in the community again next week. That is the honest trade. If your hours are worth more than the visitors they bring, paid ads are the rational choice, and this page will not pretend otherwise.
