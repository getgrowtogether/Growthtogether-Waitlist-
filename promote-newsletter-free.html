Newsletter promotion — swaps first

How to promote your newsletter for free — start with swaps

Ask newsletter writers how they actually grew and one answer comes back more than any other: other newsletters recommended them. This guide puts that route first — swaps and cross-promotion — then the directories, communities and recommendation networks that support it.

The short answer

Swaps grow newsletters — recommend a similar newsletter to your readers while it recommends yours, and repeat with new partners every week. Support that with recommendation networks, directories, guest posts and every surface you already control. Bought or traded subscribers who never open an email are worse than none.

The free routes — with their limits

Free newsletter promotion, route by route

Every route below is free. They are ordered by what they return for a new newsletter — which is why the route most guides bury in the middle stands first here.

1

Swaps and cross-promotion — the lead tactic

Find a newsletter near your size whose readers would care about yours, and agree a swap: each of you recommends the other in a dedicated section of an issue, on an agreed date, with a trackable subscribe link. A good swap sends readers who already trust the person recommending you. The honest limit: finding partners and keeping swaps fair by hand does not scale — it is the exact problem our cross-promotion platforms guide exists to explain, and the loop GrowTogether turns into a system.

2

Recommendation networks

If you publish on Substack or beehiiv, switch recommendations on: when someone subscribes to a similar publication, the platform suggests yours, and the suggestions compound as both of you grow. The honest limit: it only works between publications on the same platform, and beehiiv’s boosted placements inside its network are paid — the organic version is the free one.

3

Directories and partner-finding

List your newsletter where writers and readers browse for new ones — the places section below names them. A listing does double duty: readers find you, and so do future swap partners. The honest limit: a slow drip on its own. Directories feed the swap route; they rarely replace it.

4

Communities of writers and readers

Newsletter writers gather on Indie Hackers, in writer groups and in the reply threads of the platform you publish on. Readers gather where your topic lives. Contribute in both, share your best issue when the community’s rules allow it. The honest limit: drive-by “subscribe to my newsletter” posts are deleted everywhere and remembered. One genuinely useful post outperforms a month of them.

5

Guest spots — posts and podcasts

Write a guest post for a blog your readers already read, or be a guest on a podcast in your niche, and let the host’s audience meet you once, properly. Our podcast promotion guide covers the podcast side from the guest’s angle too. The honest limit: hosts say yes to people who pitch a topic their audience wants, not to people who pitch themselves.

6

Every surface you already control

Your social bios, your email signature, the bottom of every post you publish anywhere: one calm line pointing at the subscribe page. The honest limit: this converts people who already found you — it creates no new discovery, which is why it sits last, not first.

7

What never works: bought and traded subscribers

Bought subscribers do not open, do not reply, and drag your deliverability down for the real readers. Subscribe-for-subscribe trains are the same trade with extra steps. A list is worth the trust inside it — and trust cannot be bulk-ordered.

The first hundred

Your first 100 readers in 14 days

Fourteen days is a strong pace, not a promise — plenty of good newsletters take longer, and the sequence matters more than the calendar. What follows is the order, with the days as a rhythm to work at.

1

Days 1–2: the subscribe page and the personal invites

One subscribe page that says exactly who the newsletter is for and what arrives, a welcome email worth opening — then personal messages to everyone you know who fits. Not a blast: one person, one message. Expect this to be your first 10–30 readers, and your most loyal ones.

2

Days 3–5: get listed, get present

Submit to Lettergrowth and Reletter, switch on your platform’s recommendations, and join two writer communities properly — introduce the newsletter by sharing something useful from it, not by dropping a link.

3

Days 6–10: your first two swaps

Pick two newsletters near your size from the directories, pitch a specific swap — the section, the date, the trackable link — and publish yours on the day you promised. Report your real numbers to both partners the day after. Reliability at this size is rare, and it is remembered.

4

Days 11–14: one guest spot, then review

Pitch one guest post or podcast appearance in your niche, with the topic framed for their audience. Then look at the fourteen days honestly: which route sent readers who opened issue two? Line up next month’s swaps with the partners and places that did. The writers who reach a thousand readers are the ones still doing exactly this in month six.

Questions people search for

Newsletter promotion, answered honestly

How can I promote my newsletter for free?

Start with swaps and cross-promotion: recommend a similar newsletter to your readers while it recommends yours. Then turn on recommendation networks if your platform has them, list your newsletter in directories like Lettergrowth, show up in writer communities, and put your subscribe link on every surface you control.

What is a newsletter swap?

An agreed exchange: two newsletters of similar size and overlapping topic each recommend the other to their own readers, usually in a dedicated section of an issue. A fair swap agrees the date and placement in advance, uses a trackable subscribe link, and both sides report their real numbers afterwards. Our cross-promotion guide walks through the full method.

How do I get my first 100 newsletter subscribers?

Personally invite everyone you know who would care. List the newsletter in directories. Join the communities where your future readers and fellow writers are. Then run your first two swaps with newsletters near your size. One hundred readers reached this way open and reply; one hundred bought ones do neither.

Do newsletter recommendation networks work?

Yes, quietly. When a reader subscribes to one newsletter on Substack or beehiiv, the platform suggests similar ones, and those suggestions compound as you grow. The honest limits: they only work between publications on the same platform, and beehiiv’s boosted placements within its network are paid.

Does GrowTogether charge for newsletter promotion?

No. There are no plans and no pricing — the whole platform is free. Members contribute honest attention instead of money.
