The problem we lived

Good work, seen by nobody

GrowTogether was not started from a market report. It was started from the same week every builder knows: the work is done, the site is live — and nobody comes.

1

We built project after project

Websites, tools, apps, channels. The work was real and finished — and almost nobody ever saw it. Not because the products were weak, but because promotion was missing.

2

Promotion costs money most builders do not have

Ads, shoutouts and promotion services are priced for people who already earn. A builder with no budget has no honest way to be seen — so the work stays invisible.

3

Discovery favours the already-discovered

Search, stores and feeds show what is already popular. A first project starts with no audience, no reviews and no signal — and the system keeps it that way.

What the circle is

One circle where builders discover each other

Instead of building one more thing nobody would find, we built the thing every builder actually needs: a place where being seen no longer depends on how much money you have.

1

Put your project in the circle

Submit anything you have made — a website, an app, a SaaS, a Telegram bot or channel, a YouTube channel, an Instagram profile, a Facebook page, a podcast, a newsletter. Every listing passes a safety and quality review before it appears.

2

Discover five projects

Explore five projects from other members, matched to what you care about. Open them, try them, and leave honest feedback when you have something real to say.

3

Get discovered back

Your participation earns discovery credits, and your project enters the fair rotation — shown to members around the world, with honest numbers and never inflated ones.

How we are getting there

Waitlist first. Beta second. Public launch last.

A public launch on an empty, untested platform would burn the one first impression we get. So the circle opens in order — and the founder's own project was the first signup on the waitlist.

1

Step 1 — the waitlist

Builders join the waitlist and get their own invite link from day one. Members who bring genuine builders through their link enter the beta first — inviting earns earlier access, never credits.

2

Step 2 — the invite-only beta

The first wave opens when the first 50 members are on the waitlist — enough for every project to be genuinely discovered from day one. The first groups then test the full loop — submit, review, discover, earn credits, enter the rotation — while bugs are fixed with a forgiving crowd. The beta only passes when members come back in week two and project owners say the discovery was genuinely useful.

3

Step 3 — public launch

The doors open to everyone only after the beta proves the loop works. The free promotion guides on this site open with it — filled with real listings, not empty pages.

What it will never be

The lines we do not cross

×

Never bots or farmed attention

No auto-surfing, no tabs left open to farm credits, no fake accounts. A discovery counts only when it is a genuine session — a real person who actually stayed and looked, with real time spent and real interaction. Anything less is thrown away.

×

Never forced engagement

Nobody is made to install, like, review, subscribe or click an ad — and nobody is ever rewarded for doing those things either. A credit is earned only for discovering a project inside the circle; what a member does outside it is their own free choice, and earns nothing. Discovery is the product; pressure is not.

×

Never a promise we cannot keep

No promise of customers, sales, Google rankings or viral reach. What the circle is built to do is put your project in front of real members, in a fair rotation, so it gets a real chance to be found.

×

Never paid priority, never sold data

Nobody can buy the front of the circle, and member data is never sold. The whole platform is free — there are no plans and no pricing anywhere on GrowTogether.

Who runs it

An independent project, run by its founder

GrowTogether is an independent project built by its founder, Farzan Iqbal, who lived this problem with his own work — and who joined his own waitlist first. There is no company behind it yet, no investors, and no ads. It runs on free infrastructure and its founder’s own time — that is how it stays free. The work is done in the open: the guides on this site say plainly what each free route can and cannot do, and the ranking rules are published on the how ranking works page.
