The free routes — with their limits

Free Discord server promotion, route by route

Every route below is free, and together they answer how to promote a Discord server for free. Each one also has a ceiling — read it before you spend weeks on the wrong one. One honest note first: the demand figure on this page is a tool estimate from 2026-11-15 — "how to promote your Discord server" around 540 a month in the US, with very low measured competition — and the ranking pages for these phrases are independent sites, not big brands, which is why a genuinely better guide can compete. No volume is claimed as a fact.

1

Make the server worth joining before you promote it

Promotion sends people to a door; the server has to be worth walking through. Before you list anywhere, set clear rules, a welcome channel that says what the server is for, at least five channels people can use on day one, and one reason to stay — a weekly event, a game night, a help channel that actually answers. The honest limit: no listing fixes an empty server — the first ten members decide whether the next hundred stay, so invite friends and early fans first and get the place alive before you shout about it.

2

Disboard bumps: the free front page, every two hours

Disboard is the biggest free server list, and its bump puts your server at the top of its front page for a while — you can bump again every two hours. Write a bump message that says who the server is for in one line, not a wall of text. The honest limit: thousands of servers bump on the same timer, so a bump buys minutes of attention, not hours — it works when you bump steadily for weeks, not when you bump once and wait.

3

Partner servers: two communities, one audience each

Partner with servers your size that share your topic: you each post the other's invite in a partners channel, and members who like one community discover the other. The Discord version of cross-promotion, and the route that brings members who actually talk. The honest limit: a partnership only works when the topics truly overlap — a study server and a gaming server send each other people who leave in a day — and finding honest partners by hand does not scale.

4

The 11 free places to list your Discord server

Listing sites and Discord's own discovery list servers by category and keep sending members long after you submit — the full list with each place's limit is below. The honest limit: a listing is a signpost, not a crowd; the big lists hold tens of thousands of servers, so a listing alone never makes a server discoverable.

5

Events: give people a reason to come back this week

One event a week — a game night, a watch party, a Q and A, a build challenge — turns joiners into regulars, and regulars invite their friends. Announce it in the server and on your listing pages, then run it on time. The honest limit: an event only works if it happens again next week; one big launch night followed by silence loses more members than it gains.

6

Post where your future members already gather

Share the server where people who care about your topic already are — a Reddit community for your topic, a YouTube or TikTok clip about it, a Telegram group, a forum thread — and answer a real question well before you ever drop the invite link. A helpful post with an invite at the end gets kept; a bare link gets removed. The honest limit: every community has rules against self-promotion, and breaking them once can get you muted there for good.

7

What never works: bought members, fake activity, invite spam

Bought members are accounts that never talk — they inflate a number while the real members, the ones who chat and come back, stay flat, and a server with 5,000 members and no messages looks dead to everyone who joins. Fake activity and mass direct messages with invites get accounts flagged and servers reported. Promotion that lasts is pull: real people who chose to join.

11 free places to list your Discord server

Free Discord server listing sites, with their limits

Submit to all of them — each one is free to list on — but read the limit first, so you know what each listing can and cannot do for you.

1

Disboard

The biggest Discord server list, with a bump every two hours that puts your server on its front page. Free to list. Limit: thousands of servers bump on the same timer, so each bump buys minutes of attention.

2

top.gg

The best-known Discord listing site, for servers and bots, with voting that pushes active servers up. Free to list. Limit: votes decide the ranking, and a new server starts at the bottom of a very long list.

3

Discord.me

A simple server directory with categories and search. Free to list. Limit: a smaller audience than Disboard, and a listing is a signpost, not a crowd.

4

Discadia

A server and bot directory with category browsing. Free to list. Limit: discovery depends on your category, and crowded categories bury new servers.

5

DiscordServers.com

A long-running server list with tags and search. Free to list. Limit: a modest audience — it helps people who search by tag, and only when your tags match their words.

6

Discord Home

A server listing site with categories and a bump-style boost. Free to list. Limit: a smaller list, so it adds a trickle rather than a wave.

7

ServerHunt

A directory where people browse servers by topic and vote for favourites. Free to list. Limit: votes decide visibility, and a new server waits its turn.

8

DiscordBee

A server directory with categories, tags and search. Free to list. Limit: a niche audience of Discord regulars, not the general public.

9

Discord Street

A server and bot listing site with category pages. Free to list. Limit: a smaller audience, and a listing alone brings almost no one.

10

Reddit r/discordservers

Reddit's server-advertising community, where owners post their server with a description. Free to post. Limit: strict posting rules and a feed that moves fast — one post is gone in a day.

11

Discord Server Discovery

Discord's own in-app discovery, where users browse public servers by topic. Limit: this is not a starter route — Discord only opens Discovery to established community servers: at least 1,000 members, community features switched on, a record of real activity. Earn your way in with the other places on this list; treat Discovery as the reward, not the plan.

A plan you can actually follow

Your first 100 members in 14 days

This is how to promote a Discord server for free, step by step — how to get your first 100 members with no budget and no viral spike: about seven real members a day, from places where people are already looking for a server like yours.

1–2

Days 1–2: fix the server, then invite the first ten

Write the rules, set up the welcome channel and five usable channels, and give the server one clear reason to stay — then invite ten friends or early fans and get the first conversations going before anyone else arrives.

3–5

Days 3–5: list everywhere and pitch five partners

List the server on Disboard, top.gg, Discord.me, Discadia and the rest of the 11 places above, write one short, honest pitch, and send it to five servers your size for a partnership. Set up your first bump routine — every two hours while you are online.

6–7

Days 6–7: your first event goes live

Run the first weekly event — a game night, a watch party, a Q and A — on time, announce it in the server and on your listing pages, and post about it in two communities where you have already helped people.

8–14

Days 8–14: bump daily, one event, one post a day

Keep the bumps going, run the second weekly event, and make one honest post a day where your future members gather. At seven members a day, the first 100 arrive before the two weeks are over, and they are members who chose the server.
